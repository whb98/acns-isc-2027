Call for Papers

Call for Papers

The deployment of AI-enabled systems has created a two-sided security problem. AI models and their data, software, and deployment pipelines must resist manipulation, leakage, misuse, and adversarial failure. At the same time, AI and data-driven methods are becoming central to malware analysis, intrusion detection, vulnerability discovery, security monitoring, and incident response. These problems cut across applied cryptography, privacy, network and system security, and machine learning, making them directly relevant to the ACNS community.

The 2nd ACNS Workshop on Intelligent and Secure Computing (ACNS-ISC 2027) will provide an international forum for research in which intelligence and security are both technically substantive. The workshop centers on two complementary directions: protecting intelligent systems and using intelligent techniques to strengthen cybersecurity. It will bring together researchers and practitioners in artificial intelligence, machine learning, applied cryptography, privacy, and network and system security.

We welcome theoretical, systems, empirical, and applied contributions. The scope is deliberately focused on work in which the interaction between intelligent computing and security is central, rather than work on either area in isolation.

Topics of interest

Topics of interest include, but are not limited to:

  • Security and privacy of large language models, generative AI, multimodal models, and autonomous agents
  • Adversarial machine learning, model poisoning, backdoors, evasion, and certified robustness
  • Secure and privacy-preserving federated, distributed, and collaborative learning
  • Cryptographic mechanisms for intelligent systems, including homomorphic encryption, secure computation, zero-knowledge proofs, and verifiable computation
  • AI and machine learning for threat detection, malware analysis, vulnerability discovery, and intrusion prevention
  • Security of AI supply chains, training data, model provenance, evaluation, benchmarking, and red teaming
  • Intelligent security for IoT, cyber-physical, autonomous, cloud, and edge systems
  • Human-centered security, privacy, safety, and usability in intelligent computing
  • Reproducible studies, novel testbeds, case studies, and industrial deployments

Submissions

Full research papers, position papers describing work in progress, and papers describing novel testbeds are welcome. Submissions must be in English and follow the Springer LNCS format, using the corresponding LaTeX class.

All submissions must follow the guidelines below.

Format
PDF only; Springer LNCS format.
Page limit
At most 20 pages including the title, abstract, figures and main content, excluding references.
Review process
All submissions undergo double-blind peer review. Please prepare an anonymized manuscript.

Publication and presentation

Accepted papers will be published in the joint ACNS 2027 workshop post-proceedings in Springer’s LNCS series and will be eligible for the ACNS workshop best paper award.

At least one author of every accepted paper must register and present at the workshop in person. Each accepted paper requires a separate registration. Refer to the ACNS 2027 registration page for the applicable registration category and policies.
